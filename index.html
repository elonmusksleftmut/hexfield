Hexfield

A peer-to-peer memory layer for language models.

01

What it is

HSDM is memory that lives outside the model. A language model forgets a conversation when it ends and only sees what fits in front of it. HSDM keeps what matters somewhere else, and hands the right pieces back when they are needed.

That somewhere else is a network of peers. Each node stores its own chunks of text and retrieves them on request. A node tells the others roughly what it holds, and nothing else leaves it until a question matches. There is no central index and no server.

Where a chunk sits means something. Memory is laid out on a hex grid, and position encodes how concepts relate: neighbours are related, distance is difference. So retrieval is spatial, a walk outward from where a question lands, not just a ranking by vector similarity.

    03

    Why

    Context windows are a hard ceiling. They keep growing, but whatever does not fit is gone, and whatever fits is gone when the session ends.

    Persistent
    Memory outlasts the conversation, the session and the device.
    Distributed
    Held across many peers, not in one place that can fail or be switched off.
    Vendor-independent
    Works with whichever model you use, and stays yours when you change.
